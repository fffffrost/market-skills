# Voice of Customer Synthesis

Turn messy customer language into traceable insight without inventing representativeness.

## Start with the decision

Clarify which decision the synthesis supports: positioning, messaging, content, roadmap input, campaign design, sales enablement, or retention. Record the source mix, audience, period, and known sampling bias.

Do not summarize before defining the decision. The same quote can mean different things for messaging and product prioritization.

## Prepare the material

Preserve source identifiers such as interview code, channel, segment, date, and customer stage. Remove personal data that the deliverable does not need. Keep verbatim wording separate from analyst paraphrase.

When volume is large, normalize each observation into:

- source ID and context;
- customer situation;
- job or desired progress;
- pain, trigger, barrier, or workaround;
- outcome and emotional language;
- exact quote when useful;
- confidence or ambiguity.

Read [references/voc-template.md](references/voc-template.md) when producing a formal synthesis.

## Synthesize in five passes

### 1. Code observations

Assign concise descriptive codes close to the source language. One observation may have several codes. Do not begin with the company’s preferred narrative.

### 2. Cluster patterns

Group codes into themes only when they share the same underlying job, tension, or decision barrier. Keep superficially similar statements separate when their causes differ.

### 3. Look for structure

Compare patterns by customer segment, role, lifecycle stage, channel, product maturity, and outcome. Note contradictions and negative cases, not only majority patterns.

### 4. Build insight statements

Write each insight as:

> In [situation], [customer] wants to [make progress], but [tension/barrier], so they [behavior/workaround]. This matters because [decision implication].

Attach source count, source types, representative quotes, and confidence. Use “observed in this sample,” not population claims.

### 5. Convert insight into action

Map each strong insight to one or more actions: message to test, content question to answer, proof to add, friction to remove, segment to prioritize, sales objection to handle, or research gap to investigate.

## Output order

1. Executive readout: 3–5 decision-relevant findings.
2. Sample and evidence limitations.
3. Theme cards with quotes and confidence.
4. Segment or stage differences.
5. Action matrix with owner or next test.
6. Open questions and next research.

## Guardrails

- Never fabricate quotes, counts, or demographic attributes.
- Do not treat frequency as importance without considering severity and strategic value.
- Distinguish customer wording, analyst interpretation, and recommendation.
- Surface weak or contradictory evidence explicitly.
- Do not use this skill for statistical inference from a survey; use quantitative analysis instead.
