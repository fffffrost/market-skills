# Campaign Retrospective

Explain what happened, why it likely happened, what remains uncertain, and what the team will change next.

## Reconstruct the original contract

Recover the objective, target audience, intended behavior, strategy, channel roles, baseline, KPI definitions, budget, timing, key assumptions, and planned decision date. If these were unclear before launch, record that as a finding rather than retrofitting success criteria.

Read [references/retrospective-template.md](references/retrospective-template.md) for the formal report.

## Build one evidence table

Normalize targets, actuals, time windows, channel definitions, cost basis, tracking changes, missing values, and data source owners. Keep business outcomes, behavior signals, channel KPIs, and diagnostic metrics separate.

Do not compare numbers with different definitions or periods without an explicit note.

## Analyze in four layers

### 1. Outcome

State what met, exceeded, or missed the agreed target. Show absolute values and meaningful rates, not only percentage growth.

### 2. Funnel and segment

Locate where performance changed by stage, audience, creative, channel, period, geography, or device. Distinguish volume effects from conversion effects.

### 3. Execution

Compare plan with reality: launch timing, asset readiness, budget movement, tracking, approvals, channel delivery, sales handoff, and external events.

### 4. Explanation

Separate explanations into:

- directly observed;
- supported by several signals;
- plausible hypothesis;
- unknown and requiring a test.

Do not imply causal attribution from correlation alone.

## Convert learning into decisions

For each lesson state the evidence, confidence, scope, next action, owner, due date, and success signal. Classify it as keep, stop, change, scale, or test.

Prioritize no more than five actions. A long list without ownership is not a retrospective.

## Output order

1. Executive verdict in five lines or fewer.
2. Goal-versus-result scorecard.
3. Funnel, segment, and execution findings.
4. Explanations with confidence and attribution limits.
5. Keep / stop / change / scale / test decisions.
6. Owner, due date, and next measurement point.

## Guardrails

- Never hide missing tracking or changed KPI definitions.
- Do not cherry-pick vanity metrics to rescue a missed business objective.
- Avoid assigning blame to individuals; diagnose decisions, systems, dependencies, and evidence.
- Preserve positive and negative surprises.
- Do not use this skill for live dashboard monitoring without a completed decision period.
