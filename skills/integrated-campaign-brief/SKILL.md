# China Integrated Campaign Brief

Translate a business objective into one coordinated campaign system with a clear audience response, channel roles, deliverables, and measurement logic.

## Establish the China operating context

Confirm mainland China scope, priority cities or regions, target buyer, local language, available entities and accounts, agency or partner roles, data access, approval path, platform constraints, holidays, and regulated claims. Do not assume a global media mix or customer journey transfers directly.

Use current platform information when the plan depends on formats, targeting, account verification, advertising access, or measurement. Mark unverified platform claims and policy-sensitive steps for local owner review.

## Diagnose the request

Capture the business objective, target behavior, priority audience, timing, budget range, market, mandatory channels, available proof, constraints, and decision owner. Separate the business outcome from communication metrics.

If information is incomplete, state assumptions and build a minimum viable brief. Do not hide critical gaps behind creative language.

Read [references/campaign-brief-template.md](references/campaign-brief-template.md) for the formal deliverable.

## Build the campaign logic

### 1. Define one measurable behavior

State what the priority audience should do differently during the campaign: notice, understand, search, register, attend, trial, purchase, recommend, or re-engage. Avoid combining incompatible primary behaviors.

### 2. Identify the audience tension

Describe the audience situation, existing belief or friction, desired progress, and trigger. Support it with research or label it as a hypothesis.

### 3. Write the single-minded proposition

Express one value promise that can travel across channels. Attach the reason to believe and the proof that each channel may use.

### 4. Create the campaign idea

Develop a unifying idea, not only a title. Explain how it dramatizes the audience tension and how it can generate multiple executions without losing the proposition.

### 5. Assign channel jobs

Give each channel a distinct role such as reach, explanation, proof, participation, conversion, nurture, or amplification. Do not copy the same asset everywhere.

### 6. Plan the journey and cadence

Map preheat, launch, sustain, conversion, and follow-up. Identify the message, asset, CTA, owner, dependency, and handoff at each stage.

### 7. Define measurement

Connect business outcome, behavior signal, channel KPI, and diagnostic metric. Record baseline, target, attribution limitations, data owner, and decision date.

## Output order

1. One-page executive brief.
2. Audience and tension.
3. Proposition, proof, and campaign idea.
4. Journey, channel roles, and deliverable matrix.
5. Timeline, owners, dependencies, and risk.
6. Measurement framework and review cadence.

## Review gates

- Can a creative team produce work without guessing the strategy?
- Does every channel have a job and CTA?
- Can the audience move from exposure to the intended behavior?
- Are proof, landing destination, tracking, and owners ready?
- Is success measurable within the stated time window?

Do not use this skill for a simple content calendar with no campaign objective.
