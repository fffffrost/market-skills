# China Market Positioning

Build a positioning choice that guides product narrative and go-to-market decisions, not merely a slogan.

## Separate localization from positioning

Start by auditing the global position, but do not treat it as the answer. Reconfirm the China buyer, user, approver, procurement path, category language, local alternatives, trust barriers, distribution reality, and proof that is valid in this market.

Label each global proof point as locally usable, locally explainable, needs adaptation, or not yet valid. Never present translation fluency as market fit.

## Establish the evidence base

Collect the minimum useful inputs:

- product capabilities and actual delivery boundary;
- priority customer and buying context;
- customer jobs, pains, desired outcomes, and language;
- current alternatives and direct competitors;
- commercial model, proof, constraints, and launch stage;
- business objective and decision horizon.

Mark statements as fact, customer evidence, hypothesis, or aspiration. Do not make an aspiration sound like a shipped advantage.

Read [references/positioning-canvas.md](references/positioning-canvas.md) for a workshop or formal proposal.

## Make the strategic choices

### 1. Select the priority audience

Define the audience narrowly enough that the buying situation, urgency, and decision criteria are coherent. Distinguish user, buyer, approver, and beneficiary when they differ.

### 2. Define the frame of reference

Name the market, category, workflow, or alternative that helps the audience understand what the product replaces. Avoid inventing a category unless it improves comprehension and can be supported.

### 3. Identify the high-value promise

Choose the outcome that is important, differentiated, credible, and connected to the product mechanism. Separate functional value, business value, and emotional reassurance.

### 4. Explain the reason to believe

Connect the promise to specific capabilities, data, method, proof, customer evidence, or operating advantage. If proof is missing, write the validation needed.

### 5. State the trade-off

Good positioning excludes something. Name the lower-priority segment, use case, promise, or category association that the proposal will not lead with.

## Generate and compare routes

Create no more than three materially different routes. For each route provide:

- target audience and buying moment;
- frame of reference;
- primary promise;
- differentiation and reason to believe;
- likely objection;
- evidence strength;
- implications for product, website, content, sales, and campaign;
- what the route deliberately deprioritizes.

Recommend one route. Do not present three cosmetic phrasings as strategic alternatives.

## Deliverables

Lead with the recommended positioning statement, then provide the evidence chain, rejected alternatives, messaging hierarchy, proof gaps, and a concrete validation plan.

Use this internal statement format when useful:

> For [priority audience] in [situation], [product] is the [frame of reference] that [primary value], because [reason to believe], unlike [main alternative].

Treat it as a decision tool, not mandatory public copy.

## Guardrails

- Do not claim “唯一、第一、全链路、颠覆” without defensible evidence.
- Do not confuse a feature list, campaign slogan, mission, or company introduction with positioning.
- Do not optimize wording before the audience, frame, promise, and proof are decided.
- Surface factual and legal review needs for public claims.
- Do not use this skill when the user only wants a short tagline with no strategic decision.
